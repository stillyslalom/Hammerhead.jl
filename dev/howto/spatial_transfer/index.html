Measure a specified pipeline's spatial response

From a checkout with dependencies installed, run the fixed clean-image study:

julia --project=. bench/spatial_transfer.jl --output=bench/profile-output/spatial-transfer

The command makes sixteen CPU PIV calls: two fixed 256 × 256 px particle scenes (seeds 7321 and 7322), a translation control plus three sinusoidal transverse shears, and two complete processing schedules. Each image has particle density 0.04, Gaussian diameter 3 px (4σ), peak intensity 1, no noise and no dropout. The production point-sampling renderer and its clipped rounded square bbox are unchanged. At σ=0.75 the actual radius is ceil(3σ)=3 px; it is not a literal circular 3σ cutoff. There is no pixel-area integration or intensity normalization.

Particles in the sinusoidal cases move by the prescribed launch displacement

\[u_\mathrm{launch}=2.25+0.5\sin\left(2\pi\frac{y_\mathrm{launch}-128.5}{\lambda}+\pi/4\right), \qquad v=-1.5,\]

at wavelengths 128, 64 and 32 px. This is one discrete displacement step, not integration of a continuous trajectory through the spatially varying field. Frame B is rendered from displaced particle positions, rather than warping frame A. The constant transverse displacement gives an exact midpoint reference: y_launch = y_vector - v/2. Analytic truth is evaluated at every returned coordinate without using a measured vector to construct its own reference. No truth field supplies a processing predictor.

Both schedules start with 64 px windows/32 px overlap and 32 px windows/16 px overlap. Their last two windows are respectively [16,16] or [32,32], with overlaps 8 or 24 px. Thus both terminal grids have an 8 px stride; the 32 px wavelength has four grid samples per period, rather than being at the spatial Nyquist limit. All passes use padded cross correlation, Gaussian apodization, one peak, no outlier replacement and one sweep. The final pass enables stored random uncertainty. Predictor smoothing and the default validation settings remain part of processing. Every PIVParameters field and driver setting is recorded; this is a comparison of complete terminal-window schedules, not an isolated window-averaging kernel.

The descriptive least-squares model is intercept + s*sin(θ) + c*cos(θ), with θ from the known midpoint reference. For prescribed amplitude A=0.5, report s/A as signed in-phase response, c/A as quadrature response, hypot(s,c)/A as amplitude response and atan(c,s) as phase in radians (positive means a phase lead). The same v coefficients, normalized by the prescribed u amplitude, describe transverse response; they are not gain from a v-input harmonic. The translation control has A=0: absolute fitted coefficients and harmonic leakage are reported at all three analysis wavelengths, while normalized gain and response phase are unavailable.

Fits retain every selected sample and fail explicitly for too few observations, nonfinite arithmetic, deficient rank or poor conditioning. The relative SVD rank threshold is max(N,3)*eps(Float64)*largest_singular_value; the condition cap is 1e8. Scaling the output values before solving avoids unnecessary overflow, and coefficient/residual checks still gate availability. Fit, normalization and phase availability remain separate: a zero harmonic has no defined phase, and a failed fit never becomes zero gain. The report records rank, conditioning, sample counts and fit residuals. These are descriptive summaries, without confidence intervals based on supposedly independent nodes.

Original full-grid error/yield/UQ populations remain independent of fits. Primary origin is checked against the actual final recipe: one peak, no final replacement, one sweep and stored UQ. Masked, flagged or nonfinite vectors do not enter error metrics or fits; they remain in the appropriate yield counts. Zero sigma and component unavailability are retained. Coverage uses the full truth error, with no fitted harmonic or bias correction.

A fixed interior retains coordinates 64 ≤ x,y ≤ 192. The two terminal grids share 256 candidate interior coordinates exactly. Own-primary fits and metrics are reported separately from paired common-primary fits; shared candidates and losses to the intersection are explicit. The intersection never requires sigma availability and never interpolates a result onto another grid. Excluded analysis nodes are represented by a temporary metric mask and labeled as analysis exclusions; the stored result remains unchanged. Full-grid boundary errors are still visible.

The TOML report stores image/particle hashes, exact motion/renderer conventions, full recipes, actual diagnostics, complete populations and source/environment identity. Markdown keeps full-error/yield/coverage alongside the paired response and control leakage. Run in a fresh Julia process with frozen sources. Output guards protect sources, fixtures and unrelated existing report files; publication is ordinary two-file writing, without concurrent-writer or crash-durability guarantees. No timings or peak-memory measurements are made.

This adds a finite-frequency, finite-amplitude spatial-response observation beyond clean translation and linear shear. Two placements, one phase/amplitude, one orientation and three frequencies do not establish a universal linear transfer function, minimum resolved scale, experimental accuracy or calibrated uncertainty. Particle support, finite-frame/interpolation effects, predictor smoothing and validation remain in the measured response. Selection can vary with frequency; common accepted populations do not certify rejected nodes. Keep the original scorecard, uncertainty sweep and rendering contrast alongside this bounded study.

The frozen Windows/Julia 1.11.4 evidence run is stored at bench/profile-output/spatial-transfer-final/spatial_transfer.{toml,md}. The focused suite passed 209 checks; independent inspection of the final TOML passed 2366 population, fit, recipe, image-identity and source-stability checks. After a separate core report-validation guard changed source identities, a fresh 16-call regeneration exactly reproduced all eight scientific groups, including every original metric and fitted response. No numerical estimator or production default changed. Other-platform execution is not established.

At 32 px, own-interior primary counts were 58/256 versus 119/256 for seed 7321, and 69/256 versus 131/256 for seed 7322. The common sets therefore discard substantial populations before fitting; the gains do not describe rejected locations. Fit conditioning remained acceptable for these observed subsets, which does not remove this selection limitation. All common component sigmas were available in this run, but availability was not required to enter a fit.

Responses slightly above one at longer wavelengths are retained as observations. The control preserves full-grid errors and zero-sigma coverage separately from its small absolute harmonic leakage; no control-derived correction is applied. Finite-amplitude pipeline behavior and severe short-wavelength validation losses remain combined in this evidence. The observations do not identify a single cause, set a minimum resolved wavelength or establish calibrated uncertainty.
